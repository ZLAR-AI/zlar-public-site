Public-safe first contact

Bring one action.
Bring no confidential information.

The first conversation needs only an action class described in public-safe terms. Do not send files, internal evidence, implementation details, or information entrusted to you by another person or organization.

Safe first message

  • Your name, role, and organization.
  • One action class in abstract, public-safe terms.
  • Why that action should require accountable human authority.
  • A callback channel you are already authorized to share.
Start a public-safe email

Do not send

  • Confidential or proprietary business information.
  • Personal, customer, patient, employee, case, or government-sensitive records.
  • Credentials, tokens, private keys, vulnerabilities, security configurations, or access details.
  • Source code, internal architecture, logs, evidence packets, contracts, or attachments.

A first email is not a data room.

Do not assume an introductory email creates confidentiality protection or authorizes disclosure. Non-public material requires a separately agreed recipient, purpose, content, channel, and duration boundary.
