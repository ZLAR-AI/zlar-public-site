The idea

Real work deserves a real decision.

Machines are becoming able to do real work. Being able to do something is not the same as being allowed to do it.

People should stay in charge of the consequences that belong to them. ZLAR makes that real, while the AI stays free to read, think, plan and prepare.

Isn’t this just permissions? Here’s the difference, and exactly how the claim could fail.

A simple picture

The key stays with a person.

Imagine an AI prepares a payment. The payment can’t become real until the right person turns the right key.

Every opening leaves a signed record anyone can check.

Where this matters

Able is one thing. Allowed is another.

  • An AI with your bank login.
  • An AI that can ship software.
  • An AI that can change medical or legal records.
  • An AI that can unlock a door or move a machine.

Each of these is useful. None of them should come with the keys.

What works today

Three small, real proofs.

A test wallet that a stolen key can’t rob.

A model wallet in code, not a real bank or blockchain. Here is what happens when you run it:

The wallet holds $1,000,000. Its rule: every payment needs two signatures, the owner’s and the cosigner’s.

A normal payment: $5,000 to a supplier. Both sign. Settled.

A thief steals the owner’s key, a perfect copy, and signs a transfer of everything to himself. Not a transaction. Half a cheque.

He staples on the cosigner’s signature from the earlier payment. Still not a transaction. That signature was for different words.

The attacker takes over the AI itself and tries to stretch its $5,000 approval to $995,000. No signature. An approval is for one exact payment.

The money never moved.
